Can Trump really sell the Yosemite National Park?

30 August 2026

Short answer: not the whole park. But the real story is stranger, and it matters more than you know.

Since spring 2025, the Trump administration has reportedly been exploring ways to cede a roughly quarter-mile strip of land inside Yosemite to a company operated through Kingsbarn Realty Capital, so that this real estate firm may build an access point in the form of a road so visitors can easily reach their establishment. Without a new road, visitors to the developer's property would face a drive of an hour and a half to reach Yosemite Valley. The mechanism for the deal is a land exchange, where the firm would provide land to the Trump administration of equal value to the land given up in Yosemite. The National Park Service says it is working with the landowner on a deal that would require the landowner to buy property inside the park or in other NPS units in California. That is not a sale in the usual sense, but the result would be the same: public parkland passing into private hands.

Land laws in this case are confusing in a sense. Under U.S. federal law, the National Park Service (NPS) generally lacks the legal authority to sell or dispose of national park land, requiring explicit congressional approval and legislation. Whilst Congress declined the move (all the way back in 2025), President Trump has not been known to take no as an answer, with the evidence included in the hundreds of times that he has bypassed Congress by signing hundreds of executive orders rather than working through the legislative process. This leaves the matter in the balance, with public opinion seemingly firmly against any sale of Yosemite land, though if I had to guess, if the Trump administration really wants this exchange to go through, it will.

The most important take-away in my opinion about this fiasco is the future precedent it will set: No land is off the market.
